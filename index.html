Our Mission

Autonomy should be accountable.

As agents move from answering questions to taking action, intelligence alone is not enough. They need authority they can verify, boundaries they cannot silently expand, and records that humans and other systems can independently inspect.

◆

Human-Controlled Autonomy

Authorization, limited scopes, revocation, and approval gates for consequential actions—useful independence without unrestricted access.

✦

Cryptographic Trust

Public-key continuity, nonce-based challenges, signed messages, and provenance records to support verifiable agent interactions.

⌁

Accessible Personal AI

Local-first agents designed for modest smartphones, with optional cloud reasoning when the task requires it and the user permits it.

Give agents room to act. Keep authority visible.

A proposed infrastructure pattern for verifiable delegation: establish authority, authenticate the actor, enforce allowed scope, and record evidence. Each stage is independently testable.

Terminology matters. A signature can show control of a cryptographic key; it does not by itself prove a real-world identity, legitimacy, or safe behavior. Here, “agreements” refer to technical authorization and policy records, not substitutes for legally enforceable contracts. Human and organizational accountability remains essential.

Engineering Approach

Evidence before autonomy claims.

We pursue independent agents without treating unrestricted behavior as a virtue. Our design process defines authority, tests technical assurances, and documents limitations before increasing autonomy.

01

Define principals, authority, and threat models

Identify who may authorize actions, what an agent can access, what must require explicit approval, and how permissions can be revoked.

02

Prototype across on-device and cloud models

Prefer efficient local execution where practical; evaluate cloud models, including planned Claude API use, for complex reasoning, system engineering, and protocol development.

03

Verify signatures, policies, and outcomes

Test nonce challenges, signed artifacts, bounded delegation, recorded actions, failure cases, and resistance to misleading or malicious inputs.

04

Document, review, and iterate

Maintain versioned specifications, research notes, evaluations, and human review points. Describe what is implemented separately from what is planned.

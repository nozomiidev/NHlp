01 / The challenge

Intelligence is
only half the system.

01 — 03

When software can make decisions, use tools and collaborate with other software, every action raises practical questions. Who is acting? Who gave permission? What happened—and how can anyone check? These are infrastructure questions, not just model questions.

02 / Our approach

From autonomy
to assurance.

01 /

Establish identity.

Identify the system or cryptographic key behind an interaction. Signed challenges and records can demonstrate key continuity without pretending that a key alone establishes a person's legal identity.

02 /

Define authority.

Make delegated permissions explicit: which resources an agent may access, what it may do, when approval is required, and how access can expire or be revoked.

03 /

Verify outcomes.

Record meaningful actions and outcomes as reviewable evidence. We investigate signed messages, provenance and machine-verifiable receipts that help people inspect what agents actually did.

FIELD / 02

Trust,
with evidence.

Our protocol work explores public-key continuity checks, signed communications and verifiable interaction records. The aim is to make agent-to-agent exchanges easier to authenticate and inspect, while remaining explicit about what cryptographic proof can—and cannot—establish.

FIELD / 03 — Delegation & policy

Agreements
systems can respect.

We study machine-readable permissions, approval conditions, revocation and action records as practical boundaries between people and autonomous software. These technical mechanisms complement—not replace—human oversight and legal agreements.
